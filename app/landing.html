A complete guidance moment

From question to next step.

The demo centers on a real public-benefit question and shows how the assistant handles uncertainty and directs people to an official source.

01

Ask in everyday language

The caller asks a practical PM-KISAN question in Hindi or English using an ordinary phone.

02

Clarify only what matters

BadaBhai asks a short follow-up when it needs context, then explains likely next steps in plain language.

03

Repeat and offer the official route

The caller can ask for key details again. Personal eligibility and application status go to the official government channel.

Built for guidance, not decisions. The demo shares general information. It does not determine eligibility, access government records, or claim government endorsement.
Why access matters

AI should work for the phone people already have.

Voice can reduce the barriers created by device cost, mobile data, reading confidence, app setup and unfamiliar digital services.

01

Reach beyond smartphones

A call-based interface can bring guided help to feature-phone users and people with limited internet access.

02

Make complex steps clearer

People can ask follow-up questions, hear information repeated, and move at their own pace in a familiar conversation.

03

Connect people to services

With approved local information, public agencies could explain schemes and route callers to verified offices and application channels.

A path to public-service access

Built to grow with local partners.

A future rollout could be configured for a country's languages, public programs, accessibility needs and official support channels. Each program would need trusted, current sources and a clear route to a human or government office.

For Meta, BadaBhai demonstrates how Meta's AI models can support voice-first access on familiar devices, and highlights the local-language, low-bandwidth needs that inclusive AI products must serve. This is an independent hackathon prototype, not a Meta or government service.
