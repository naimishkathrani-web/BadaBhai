Why access matters

AI should work for the phone people already have.

Voice can reduce barriers created by device cost, mobile data, reading confidence, app setup and unfamiliar digital services. GSMA estimates about 200 million new feature phones sold globally in 2024; India shipments were about 54 million, attributed to IDC.

01

Reach beyond smartphones

A call-based interface can bring guided help to feature-phone users and people with limited internet access.

02

Make complex steps clearer

People can ask follow-up questions, hear information repeated, and move at their own pace in a familiar conversation.

03

Connect people to answers

With approved information sources, guidance can be grounded and routed to verified next steps.

Open future-potential video ↗
01

Explain everyday topics simply

Maths, science and how-to guidance on a feature phone, in Hindi, English, Swahili, Bahasa Indonesia and more as each language is validated.

02

Help governments reach people

With verified sources, public agencies could explain program purpose, required documents, deadlines and official helplines in local languages.

03

Explore better speech models

With separate opt-in, strong anonymization and local governance, speech in Bhojpuri, Tamil, Marathi, Swahili, Hausa, Bahasa Indonesia and Tagalog could support research on voice-token models, language control, native prosody and code-switching. This could inform Meta research for more natural multilingual AI, but the demo does not train models or use calls for training.

04

Replace long enterprise IVR menus

Callers could describe an issue such as “My bill seems too high” and reach the right self-service path or staff team. Account changes still require authorization.

05

Enable voice orders from villages

Local marketplaces could take voice orders for groceries or farm supplies and connect them to logistics beyond cities.

06

Translate live calls

Future speech systems could help a farmer speaking Hindi talk with an English-speaking buyer, if accuracy, latency and consent are handled well.

These are future possibilities that require approvals, infrastructure, consent and local partnerships. BadaBhai today is an independent hackathon prototype, not a Meta or government service.
